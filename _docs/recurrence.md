# Recurrence consumer contract

Cross-context consumers import the Planning recurrence contracts from `pin.contexts.planning.api`; `recurrence_api.py` is the internal feature export module. A caller supplies the authenticated owner, the Identity and Billing ports on the same caller-owned unit of work, a clock, a `SqlAlchemySeriesStore`, and a required typed confirmation verifier. The verifier must be backed by a trusted preview producer. There is no boolean-confirmed mode or permissive default verifier.

## Preview and creation

Construct `RecurringPlanDraft` and `RecurrenceRule` with a positive interval and exactly one supported frequency: daily, weekly with an explicit weekday tuple, or monthly with an explicit day-of-month or last-day pattern. A positive count and an end date are both optional finite stop conditions; without either, the series continues until explicitly stopped. Creation previews, generation requests, and exception previews remain bounded by their configured date horizons and occurrence batch limits. For monthly day patterns, `monthly_day` remains immutable when an occurrence clamps to the last day of a shorter month, so January 31 projects February 28/29 and then March 31.

Read the current `PreferencesSnapshot`, then call `preview_recurring_series(owner_id, draft, expected_preferences_version, default_zone, default_reminders, at)`. It returns a bounded `RecurrencePreview` with `through`, `projection`, and `approval_projection_sha256`. Show the user every projected local date/time and zone, the UTC instant, reminder instants/offset provenance, any default reminders omitted as already past, and DST resolution notices before issuing the proof. A recurring nonexistent local time is shifted forward by exactly the DST gap. A repeated hour chooses the earlier fold and records that choice in the preview and stored occurrence.

The trusted producer issues `RecurrenceConfirmationProof` bound to the authenticated owner, its preview ID and version, producer identity, expiry, and the exact `approval_projection_sha256`. The digest includes the typed recurrence fields, effective zone/reminders, preference version, horizon, ordered occurrence dates and local/UTC schedule meaning, reminder resolutions/omissions, recurrence keys, and DST decisions; it excludes generated database IDs and processing timestamps. `create_confirmed_series` checks the proof, locks and refreshes preferences, obtains fresh access at the write boundary, and rebuilds the bounded preview. If the approved projection changed, the command is rejected as stale and the caller's transaction rolls back proof consumption. Creation of the plan item, series, occurrences, reminders, receipt, and versioned outbox events commits together.

The default preview is bounded to a 90-day horizon and at most 32 occurrences. The initial window covers through the later of today plus the horizon or the requested start date plus the horizon, so a future-start series receives a useful first projection. Consumers may choose a smaller supported preview bound, but must issue the proof for the returned exact projection.

## Generation and durable identity

`generate_recurrence_horizon(GenerateRecurrenceSeries(...))` is a bounded internal application operation, not a user-visible confirmation flow. It receives the Identity port on the caller's unit of work, locks current preferences, and requires them to match the series' stored approved preference version and quiet policy. If preferences changed, generation fails until a newly confirmed future projection updates the series policy; generation never stamps today's version onto an older approval. It advances a locked durable cursor and creates at most 1000 occurrences per request (the default and hard maximum are both 1000) through a date no more than 366 days ahead. The unique `(series_id, recurrence_key)` and occurrence sequence constraints are the final duplicate guard; a restarted worker resumes from the persisted cursor. A caller that loses an optimistic version race reloads the `SeriesSnapshot` and retries with its current version. Generated reminder offsets are resolved by Planning and then passed through the same Notifications quiet-hours policy as preview and exception projections. Generated occurrences are ordinary Planning occurrences, each with its own ID, state, approved preference version, final reminder rows, and versioned outbox event.

The Planning `SqlAlchemyPlanningScheduleSnapshotStore.lock_schedule` reader (and its Notifications bridge `SqlAlchemyReminderScheduleStore.lock_current_schedule`) is a same-unit-of-work, locked view of the current durable schedule. It reads current Identity preferences first, then a series where applicable, followed by its materialized occurrences in stable order. Snapshots include terminal rows, state/version fences, exact stored reminder due instants and provenance, the preference version approved for each occurrence (`None` for legacy rows without evidence), and series state/version. A legacy `None` is not upgraded by inference and cannot authorize reminder intents.

## Exceptions and stopping

Owner-confirmed weekly mutation policy (SPEC-001 v0.19, TR-02): a this-and-future date edit replaces only the selected occurrence's weekday in the future weekday set. Monday/Wednesday moved from Monday to Tuesday becomes Tuesday/Wednesday; it does not rotate both days. The mutation producer must supply that resulting pattern through the existing `new_pattern` contract, and display its weekdays and affected future dates before confirmation. The existing finite count, prior-history and interval-reanchoring invariants still apply. This policy does not change monthly pattern rules.

`preview_recurrence_exception` produces a typed `RecurrenceExceptionPreview`; show its exact changed fields, approved rule, bounded affected future window, and remaining-count value (which is `None` for an unbounded series), then issue a proof bound to `approval_projection_sha256`. Dates beyond that window continue from the newly approved recurrence rule when generation resumes. An omitted reminder list inherits the series' existing reminder behavior; an explicitly supplied list, including an empty list, changes reminders for the selected future segment. `apply_confirmed_recurrence_exception` rechecks owner, series version, preferences, access, proof, and the exact projection inside the caller's transaction. A single-occurrence exception keeps the recurrence rule and all other occurrence identities. A this-and-future change anchors the new interval at the selected new date, carries any finite remaining count forward, and leaves completed history and its IDs untouched. The requested weekday or monthly pattern is part of the approved projection. The operation rejects a change that would rewrite terminal future history rather than reopen it. Each rescheduled future occurrence keeps its ID and recurrence key while its ordinary occurrence version advances; the immutable exception audit and outbox records commit atomically.

`preview_stop_series` returns the exact series version and open generated occurrences that will remain unchanged. After that projection is approved, `stop_confirmed_series` checks `CANCEL_PLAN` access at both proof/application boundaries and records the stopped state with a versioned outbox event. This preserves the owner's right to stop future generation after ordinary creation access expires; a valid current confirmation proof is still required. A stopped series cannot generate further occurrences. This transition only stops series generation and does not implement #15's full user cancellation flow or alter already generated occurrence states. Neither exception nor stop API implements capture, user-interface, coaching, or notification delivery behavior.

Before writing an exception, the store locks and refreshes every affected occurrence in sequence order and checks its current open state, version, and stable identity against the approved snapshot. A concurrent completion, cancellation, or version change rejects the stale command; transaction rollback leaves the proof unconsumed and commits no exception, receipt, or new outbox event.

All adapters leave transaction ownership to the composition layer. Session sharing is composed externally; the recurrence store does not create or commit a database session. The typed contracts intentionally leave the future capture/mutation preview producer to its owning feature while making its required proof fields and displayed projection explicit here.
