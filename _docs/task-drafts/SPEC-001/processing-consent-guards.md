## Increment and context

Only newly admitted work with the current explicit grant can reach AI processing or produce an AI-derived effect. Withdrawal prevents later protected commits and dispatches, while accurately preserving already-started attempts and their costs. Deletion/return and restored snapshots cannot resurrect old consent or AI artifacts. These are one coupled authorization boundary: do not accept a subset as complete while another worker/replay/restore route can bypass it.

## Scope

- Stamp current grant provenance at trusted ingress before AI-eligible durable admission. The stamp is server-owned and immutable on duplicate receipt. Missing/legacy/stale stamps deny AI and complete once with a localized consent prompt/resend instruction; later acceptance cannot restamp or replay old input.
- Guard text/advice/clarification, voice reservation/download/local recognition/stored-transcript continuation, every external model attempt/repair/direct entry, cached result storage/replay, result/proposal/proof application, AI-derived Save/Confirm and reply dispatch. Keep deterministic commands/previews/reminders on their existing authorization paths.
- Use Identity's same-UoW final fence at every protected commit/marker and preserve grant/policy provenance through capture, conversation results/provider calls, proposed mutation proofs and AI reply payloads. Add only the migration/DTO fields needed for these boundaries. Legacy artifacts fail closed.
- Terminalize stale queued work, suppress late/obsolete AI output/proofs/replies and release unused holds using existing Billing semantics. Denial before first dispatch means zero total calls; later denial means zero additional calls. Preserve started/unknown provider receipts and do not equate provider costs with user-turn charges.
- Integrate operational consent purge, same-canonical-owner return fences and idempotent restored-grant invalidation before recovery release. Prevent older workers or rollback from bypassing enforcement; document the compatible-release/fenced-rollback boundary.

### Concrete transaction lock order and entry ownership

This is the common predecessor/consumer handoff for PCR-P01. `F(owner)` is Identity's published wrapper around `lock_action(session, owner_id, "identity.processing_consent")`: one stable key for grant/revoke/authorization/purge/invalidation, held by PostgreSQL until the caller transaction commits or rolls back, including when the consent row is absent. It creates no receipt/history row. `J` is the existing `lock_job_for_effects` lock on the live Job and, when applicable, its ConversationLease. `L` is the existing Billing account-lifecycle lock. `T` means the subsequent owned target/effect rows listed below, not a new lock service.

For a participating owner transaction the common acquisition order is **F → J (if it has a claim) → L (if that operation uses lifecycle/access) → T**. Acquire F at the outer transaction entry, before any of those prelocks; acquire J explicitly before code that previously obtained it through a later receipt call. Rechecks/reentrant acquisition of already-held locks are allowed, but a lower helper must not newly acquire F after an outer caller has taken J/L/T. Do not hold two owners' F locks in one transaction. The map below fixes the concrete entry changes; it does not leave the order for the Executor to design. Existing internal target-row order is retained within a single owner after the common prefix; the shared F prevents same-owner participating target sequences from interleaving. No path may wait for a different owner's F while holding this prefix.

**Ledger export and recovery ordering:** `export_tombstones` stays a separate transaction/phase that locks only tombstones, appends the existing local artifact and commits; it neither acquires F nor subsequently enters lifecycle/purge. Therefore it cannot hold a tombstone and wait for F. Do not move filesystem I/O under F. Read-only candidate/artifact scans hold no owner locks. For restored invalidation, the outer recovery composition first arms/checks the exact recovery generation under the global gate row and commits; while runtime remains fenced it invalidates one owner at a time in F → Identity-target transactions; a separate gate-row transaction records/verifies completion for that exact generation before release. Never hold the global recovery row while waiting for an owner's F, nor acquire the global row while holding F. A matching already-fenced generation resumes idempotently; a different generation or released runtime cannot reuse the invalidation completion. Platform keeps generic gate ownership and calls the outer composed Identity capability rather than importing its ORM. Guards owns these recovery/CLI/script adjustments; Identity supplies the invalidation port. Existing deletion/delivery/payment/usage checks are retained.

**Preserved nonparticipating paths:** claim/renew/defer/complete scheduling transactions that touch only Job/ConversationLease do not acquire F or L/T afterward, so they cannot form a reverse F wait. Pure provider-usage receipt reconciliation retains its late financial-fact behavior: it locks provider receipt rows only and must not call consent authorization, job or lifecycle acquisition afterward. The participating result-storage/dispatch and allowance UoWs are explicitly covered above. Pure reads and ledger export likewise never request F after their own work. External transport/inference runs after commit with no locks. Shared deterministic application/reply/reminder entries do take F for ordering as specified, but never require a grant; this is the bounded adjustment needed to keep the participating deletion and dispatch routes compatible, not a general locking refactor. Retain all existing claim-token, lifecycle, idempotency and unknown-outcome checks.

A committed provider or Telegram dispatch-attempt marker defines already-started work. Its subsequent send may finish after withdrawal, but a new repair/dispatch cannot start on the obsolete tuple. An AI-derived result/effect that committed before withdrawal remains historical; any subsequent AI-derived Save or not-yet-started reply must obtain a fresh final authorization for its original tuple. Regrant changes the grant ID and cannot validate old artifacts. A deterministic saved-plan Done/Cancel/reminder is not automatically classified as AI-derived merely because that plan was originally created with AI.
